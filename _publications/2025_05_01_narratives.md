This paper explores the effect of substantial shifts in political narratives on attitudes towards migration in the US. We document a marked increase in the share of political advertising aired on TV that addresses the topic of migration, from 1% in 2012 to 12% in 2018 of all ads shown. Exploiting the geographic segmentation of media markets and timing of the advertisements, we study the causal effects of political advertising on various outcomes, including campaign contributions, public resonance on Twitter and election outcomes.
