In regression discontinuity designs covariates can be imbalanced in the samples used for estimation, even if their distribution is continuous in the running variable. I demonstrate that under such covariate imbalance the standard local polynomial RD estimator (base RD) implicitly compares units with different covariate values. This induces an additive term in the bias of the base RD estimator, which I refer to as composition bias. To reduce composition bias, I argue that researchers should adjust for pre-treatment covariates so that the estimator compares only units with similar covariate values. Specifically, I propose the Stratified RD estimator and prove that it has asymptotically weakly lower variance and worst-case bias than the base RD estimator, which leads to shorter bias-aware confidence intervals for the average treatment effect at the cutoff. In a simulation calibrated to replication data from a study of a merit-based financial aid program in Colombia, stratifying on parental education reduces the bias relative to the base RD estimator by 14–23%, and stratifying on both parental education and whether a student lives in a metropolitan area reduces it by 25–30%, depending on the bandwidth.
