When a policy varies discontinuously across geographic space, researchers often identify its effects by comparing outcomes on either side of the policy border in a spatial regression discontinuity design. For estimation, most regression specifications control for location along the border using border-segment fixed effects, and for location away from the border using a bandwidth and sometimes linear adjustments in distance. We clarify the conditions under which segment fixed effects are needed, and propose a modeling technique to characterize the benefits and costs of fixed effects in finite samples. We provide theoretical and practical guidance: Many prior studies chose border segments that are likely longer than optimal, and a common local linear adjustment for distance to the border can yield distant comparisons. We illustrate the proposed auxiliary analyses and robustness checks by reanalyzing empirical studies.
