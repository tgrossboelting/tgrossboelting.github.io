In many Regression Discontinuity Designs researchers observe the outcomes of units before and after treatment, and outcomes generally depend on the running variable as well as unobserved covariates. Such unobservables can increase not only the variance of the standard local polynomial RD estimator but also its bias if they are imbalanced within the estimation sample. This paper demonstrates that differencing post- and pre-treatment outcomes eliminates the contribution of time-invariant unobservables to the outcome under a separability condition. As a result, estimating the average treatment effect at the cutoff using the differenced outcome rather than the post-treatment outcome weakly reduces worst-case bias and can also reduce variance. Differencing can thus lead to substantially shorter bias-aware confidence intervals, depending on the correlation of the outcome over time and the researcher's assumptions about how much bias is caused by time-invariant unobservables.
